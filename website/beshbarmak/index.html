Beshbarmak

Бешбармак — literally "five fingers," eaten by hand from a shared platter

Serves 6–8 Prep 45 min Cook 2 hr 30 min Difficulty Medium

Beshbarmak is the centrepiece of every Kyrgyz celebration. A whole bone-in lamb is simmered for hours until the broth runs amber and the meat falls apart. Wide, silky noodles called kesme are cooked in that same broth, then layered on a large plate with the pulled meat and crowned with rings of onion softened in yet more broth. A bowl of the pure broth, called shorpo, is served alongside for sipping.

Ingredients

For the lamb and broth

  • 1.5 kg bone-in lamb shoulder or ribs, cut into large pieces
  • 3 litres cold water
  • 1 large onion, halved
  • 1 head of garlic, halved crosswise
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tsp whole black peppercorns
  • Salt to taste

For the kesme noodles

  • 300 g (2¼ cups) plain flour, plus extra for dusting
  • 2 eggs
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) warm water
  • ½ tsp salt

For the onion topping

  • 3 medium onions, thinly sliced into rings
  • 250 ml (1 cup) strained lamb broth
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • Small bunch of fresh dill or parsley, chopped

Method

  1. Place the lamb pieces in a large pot, cover with cold water, and bring to a boil. Skim all grey foam thoroughly for the first 10 minutes.
  2. Add the halved onion, garlic, bay leaves, and peppercorns. Reduce heat to a gentle simmer, partially cover, and cook for 2 to 2½ hours until the meat is very tender and pulling away from the bone.
  3. While the lamb cooks, make the noodles. Combine flour, eggs, water, and salt in a bowl and knead for 8–10 minutes until smooth and firm. Wrap in cling film and rest at room temperature for 30 minutes.
  4. Divide the dough into four portions. On a lightly floured surface, roll each portion very thin (about 1–2 mm). Cut into wide diamonds or rectangles roughly 8 × 5 cm. Dust with flour to prevent sticking.
  5. Remove the cooked lamb from the pot. Strain the broth; discard the spent aromatics. Season the broth generously with salt.
  6. Pull the meat from the bones in large chunks. Keep warm.
  7. In a small saucepan, simmer the sliced onions in 250 ml of the strained broth for 5–7 minutes until translucent and tender. Season with black pepper.
  8. Bring the remaining broth to a rolling boil. Cook the noodles in batches for 3–4 minutes until just tender. Remove with a slotted spoon.
  9. Arrange the noodles on a large round platter. Top with the pulled lamb, then spoon the onion and its broth over everything. Scatter fresh herbs. Serve immediately, with bowls of hot broth on the side.
Tip: The key to a clear, flavourful broth is patient skimming at the very start and a low, lazy simmer throughout — never a vigorous boil.
