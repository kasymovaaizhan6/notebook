Manti

Манты — plump steamed dumplings, eaten hot with sour cream or chilli oil

Makes 30–35 Prep 1 hr Cook 40 min Difficulty Medium

Manti are steamed in a special multi-tiered pot called a mantovarka. Unlike Chinese dumplings, the dough is unleavened and rolled thin; the filling is raw and finely chopped by hand, never minced by machine, so the texture stays juicy. A pinch of cumin and plenty of raw onion are non-negotiable. Serve with a spoonful of kaymak (thick cream) or tangy suzme (strained yoghurt).

Ingredients

For the dough

  • 400 g (3 cups) plain flour, plus extra for dusting
  • 1 egg
  • 180 ml (¾ cup) warm water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp neutral oil

For the filling

  • 500 g lamb shoulder, finely hand-chopped (or coarse-ground)
  • 3 medium onions, very finely diced
  • 100 g lamb fat or beef suet, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • ½ tsp ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp cold water

To serve

  • Sour cream, suzme, or kaymak
  • Chilli flakes or chilli oil (optional)
  • Fresh dill

Method

  1. Make the dough: whisk together the egg, warm water, salt, and oil. Add to the flour and mix until shaggy, then knead on a clean surface for 10 minutes until smooth and elastic. The dough should feel slightly stiffer than pasta dough. Cover with a damp cloth and rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Make the filling: combine the chopped lamb, fat, onion, cumin, pepper, salt, and cold water in a bowl. Mix well with your hands — the onion should be worked in so it releases its moisture into the meat. Refrigerate until needed.
  3. Divide the dough into four pieces. Roll each piece on a floured surface to about 2 mm thick. Cut into 10–11 cm squares.
  4. Place a generous tablespoon of filling in the centre of each square. Bring opposite corners together and pinch to seal, then pinch the remaining two corners up and press all edges firmly closed. Traditional manti have a rose-shaped top crimp, but any tight seal will do.
  5. Oil the steamer trays generously to prevent sticking. Arrange the manti with at least 1 cm between each. Steam over vigorously boiling water for 35–40 minutes — do not lift the lid during cooking.
  6. Serve immediately from the steamer with a dollop of sour cream, a scatter of dill, and chilli oil for those who want heat.
Tip: The fat in the filling is what makes manti juicy, not dry. Don't skip it or substitute lean mince — the steam renders it into a small pool of savoury broth inside each dumpling.
