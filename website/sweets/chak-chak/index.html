Chak-chak

Чак-чак — crispy fried dough mounded in honey, the centrepiece of every celebration table

Serves 10–12 Prep 30 min Cook 40 min Difficulty Easy

Chak-chak is the sweet that appears at weddings, Nowruz feasts, and any gathering where something golden and celebratory is needed. Short strands of egg dough are fried until pale gold and crunchy, then tossed while hot in warmed honey and pressed into a mound or pyramid that sets firm as it cools. Every family shapes it differently — some pile it high in a dome, others press it flat in a dish — but the flavour is always the same: pure honey sweetness with a satisfying, shattering crunch.

Ingredients

For the dough

  • 3 eggs
  • 2 tbsp vodka or brandy (helps keep the dough crisp)
  • 1 tbsp sour cream or kefir
  • Pinch of salt
  • 280–320 g (2–2¼ cups) plain flour, plus extra for dusting

For frying and the honey coat

  • Vegetable oil for deep-frying (about 1 litre)
  • 300 g (about 1 cup) good-quality honey
  • 2 tbsp sugar (optional, deepens the caramel note)

Method

  1. Whisk together the eggs, vodka, sour cream, and salt in a large bowl until well combined.
  2. Gradually stir in the flour until a soft dough forms. Turn out onto a lightly floured surface and knead for 5–6 minutes until smooth. The dough should be slightly firmer than pasta dough. Wrap and rest for 20 minutes.
  3. Divide the dough into four pieces. On a lightly floured surface, roll each piece into a thin sheet about 3–4 mm thick. Cut into strips about 6 mm wide, then cut each strip into short pieces 3–4 cm long — roughly matchstick-sized fingers.
  4. Spread the pieces on a floured tray and let them dry at room temperature for 10 minutes. This prevents them from sticking during frying.
  5. Pour oil into a deep heavy-based pan to a depth of at least 8 cm and heat to 170°C (340°F). Fry the dough pieces in small batches, stirring gently, for 2–3 minutes until pale gold and just crisp. Do not let them brown deeply — they should look like short golden straws. Drain on paper towels and cool completely before coating.
  6. When all the dough is fried, combine the honey and sugar (if using) in a small saucepan over medium heat. Warm, stirring, until the sugar dissolves and the honey is loose and just beginning to bubble at the edges. Do not boil hard.
  7. Pour the warm honey over the fried pieces in a large bowl and fold quickly with a wooden spoon until every piece is coated.
  8. Wet your hands with cold water and shape the mixture into a mound or pyramid on a plate. Work quickly, as it sets fast. Leave to cool and firm for at least 30 minutes before serving.
  9. To serve, break pieces off the mound at the table — it should shatter in shards of honey-lacquered dough.
Tip: The dough must be completely cool and the honey just warm (not boiling) when they meet — hot honey on hot dough makes the pieces go soft and they will never set properly. Fry ahead, cool fully, then coat at the last moment.
